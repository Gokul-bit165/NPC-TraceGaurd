The problem

Attackers need one mistake. Defenders see it as two unrelated, low-priority alerts.

1

A phishing email arrives

“Your password expires today, verify now.” The mail tool scores it Medium and moves on.

2

The user types their password

Nobody sees this step. It is the moment the account is lost.

3

The attacker signs in from another country

The login tool scores it Medium. Two Mediums sit in two queues, hours apart.

TraceGuard2
Our solution

TraceGuard joins the two alerts into one graded case, and explains every point of the score.

1

Collect

Reads new mail from a connected Gmail inbox (read-only) every 5 minutes, and takes sign-in logs. Cleans and de-duplicates them automatically.

2

Connect

Scores the email and the sign-in separately. If the same user has a risky email and then a risky login within 72 hours, the case score rises.

3

Decide

Shows the call, the confidence, the evidence and ordered next steps in plain language. A person approves every action.

The agent process

Seven roles, one job each. Only one of them uses a language model.

An “agent” here is a role with one task and a checked hand-off, not a free-running AI. The Narrator can only write text; it cannot change a score.

A case, worked through

Two Medium-looking signals become one High or Critical case.

Scripted scenario (one of nine)

A student gets a risky email. Forty minutes later their account signs in from a new country on a new device.

Email evidence90
Login evidence57
Case score100

score = min(100, email + 0.5 × login) = min(100, 90 + 28.5) → Critical. The formula and every cap are shown on the case page.

Live run on a real Gmail inbox

  1. Real mail “URGENT: Your College Account Will Be Suspended Within 2 Hours!” fetched by n8n.
  2. Emails scored Medium: the URGENT mail 30 (wording), and a marketing mail 36 (reply-to mismatch), the strongest in the window.
  3. Simulated sign-in from Lagos, three failures then a success, 35 minutes later.
  4. Case became High 75, correlated: “Likely account compromise after phishing”.
  5. Decision: verify with the user, then contain if unconfirmed, 79% confidence.

Why not Critical? Without a confirmed second signal, TraceGuard asks the user before any lockout.

The point: alone, each signal was only Medium. Joined, the case moved up and the analyst saw one page that says why.

The AI, tested on mail it never saw

Real curves from saved test scores. Test: 1,204 phishing (Nazario) + 5,000 legitimate (CEAS_08), sources absent from training.

We compared three models and shipped the simplest one that won.

  • Why it ships: best of the shippable models, 2.6 MB, about 0.09 s per email on a CPU, and its top words explain each result.
  • Honest limit: on a new mail source the false-alarm rate was 3.9%, not 1%. Expect some false alarms on unfamiliar legitimate mail.
  • Only 1,243 real phishing training examples after de-duplication; the rest of the positive class is limited.

Why a security team can trust it

Built so the AI never has the last word.

Every point is traceable

The score is a ledger of named signals with points and caps. Nothing is a black box.

A language model may only explain

It receives structured evidence, not email text. Its answer is checked against the ledger; on any mismatch the plain template is used.

People approve every action

Containment steps (7 of 7 in the playbook) wait for an analyst. Each is marked reversible or not.

Read-only and non-destructive

Gmail access is read-only. TraceGuard never deletes, blocks or sends mail on its own.

We audited our own data

Removed advertising spam from the “phishing” class, removed near-duplicates, and split by source so the test is honest.

Tested

142 automated tests pass, including nine scripted attack scenarios that must land in their expected band.

Honest status

What is real, what is simulated, what comes next.

Built and working

  • Real Gmail ingest through n8n, automatic every 5 minutes
  • Email scoring, per-email investigation, mailbox view
  • Cases, correlation, decision panel, response playbook
  • Reports, audit log, report export
  • 142 automated tests

Limited or simulated

  • Sign-in logs are simulated (no identity provider yet)
  • Login scoring is rules, not a trained model
  • Text model trained on older public mail; 3.9% false alarms on a new source
  • 10% of harmless generated campus messages flagged by the text model alone

Next

  • Connect real sign-in logs (Google Workspace or Entra)
  • Retrain on labelled campus mail
  • Several mailboxes at once
  • Slack / Teams / email alerts through n8n
  • Campus pilot

Straight answers

Is the data real? The mail is real Gmail. The sign-ins are generated and labelled.   Does the AI decide? No. It scores; a person approves.   Can it harm a mailbox? No: read-only access, no automatic deletion or blocking.
