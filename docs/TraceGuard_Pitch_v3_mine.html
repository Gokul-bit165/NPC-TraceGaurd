The problem

Attackers tell a story. Our tools each see one chapter.

1

A phishing mail arrives

"Your account will be suspended in 2 hours." The mail filter scores it Medium. It might be a newsletter.

2

The password is typed in

Nothing visible happens. The user may not even know.

3

A sign-in from a new country

After several failed tries. The login monitor scores it Medium. Could be travel.

What goes wrong today

  • Two Medium alerts in two tools. Nobody links them, so both get ignored.
  • Analysts see hundreds of alerts and have no time to dig.
  • A score with no reason cannot be trusted, so it is not acted on.

Who feels it

Students and staff lose their account and data.
Small IT teams cannot watch every inbox and login.
The institution pays in downtime, cleanup and trust.

Problem2 / 13
Our solution

Evidence in. One graded, explained case out.

1 · Collect

n8n reads Gmail (read-only) every 5 minutes and on a Sync button. Sign-ins arrive the same way.

2 · Analyse

Each mail and each sign-in is scored. Every point is tied to a reason you can read.

3 · Connect

A suspicious mail followed by a suspicious sign-in within 72 hours becomes one case, scored higher than either alone.

4 · Decide

A recommended call, options, and ordered steps. A person approves anything risky.

Why this is different

The score is a sum of reasonsNo black box. Click a reason code and see the evidence behind it.
The language model only explainsIt cannot change a score. Its text is checked against the evidence, or a fixed template is used.
Humans stay in chargeNothing is blocked, deleted or reset on its own. Each step shows if it can be undone.
Runs on one laptopDocker, a local database and free tools. No paid cloud.

End-to-end flow

From an email arriving to a human decision, in seven steps.

1
A mail reaches the inbox
Real Gmail message, for example “URGENT: Your College Account Will Be Suspended Within 2 Hours!”
2
n8n fetches it
On a 5-minute schedule or the Sync now button. Read-only. Each Gmail ID is remembered so nothing is scored twice.
3
n8n prepares it
Rebuilds a standard email with the headers (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), body and links the checker needs.
4
TraceGuard scores the mail
Sender checks, link checks, wording model. Result: 30, Medium with each reason listed.
5
Sign-ins are scored
Each sign-in is compared with that user's own normal: country, device, hours, failed tries.
6
The two are linked
A bad sign-in within 72 hours of a bad mail makes one case: High 75, “Likely account compromise after phishing”.
7
Explain, recommend, approve
Plain-language story, a recommended call, ordered steps. The analyst approves; every action is logged.

How a score is built

The score is a sum of reasons. Two Mediums make a High.

The formula
Case = min(100, max(E, L) + 0.5 × min(E, L))

E = email score, L = login score. The weaker signal still adds half its weight.

Worked example (screen shown later)

Email 90, login 57. Uplift = 0.5 × 57 = 29. Total 90 + 29 = 119, capped at 100, Critical. Each of the 100 points is a line in the ledger.

Guard rails

A mail alone is capped at 79, so it never reaches Critical without a matching sign-in. Each signal group has a cap so one noisy rule cannot take over.

Risk bands
Low 0–29Auto-closed, kept as baseline
Medium 30–59Alert the user, watch
High 60–79Verify with user, then contain
Critical 80–100Contain now, with approvals
What the scorers look at

Mail: reply-to mismatch, link text vs target, risky domains, SPF/DKIM/DMARC, urgency and wording, hidden text.
Sign-in: new country or device, odd hours, failed bursts, success after failures.

Shipped model, held-out sources

Precision 0.84 · Recall 0.87 · False alarms 3.9% · AUC 0.976 · about 90 ms per email on a CPU.

Why we trust the pipeline

  • The model explains itself with its own top terms.
  • Hidden text and “mark this as safe” tricks inside an email are detected and scored as an attack.
  • The language model sees only structured evidence, never raw email text, and its answer is checked against the ledger.

What we do not claim

Training mail is public and old (2000–2008). About 10% of harmless generated campus messages are flagged by wording alone. The login scorer is rules, not machine learning.

Product9 / 13
Safety and responsibility

Built so a wrong answer is cheap to catch and undo.

Read-only by design

Gmail access is the read-only scope. TraceGuard cannot send, delete or move your mail.

Human approval

Risky steps (block, purge, reset) wait for an analyst. Steps are ordered: sessions are revoked before a password reset.

Reversible first

Each step is labelled reversible or not. Warnings and quarantine come before anything permanent.

Audit trail

Who approved what, and when, is logged with the case.

Roles

Admin, analyst and user see different things. A user can only open their own alerts.

Privacy

Data stays in a local database. Keys sit in a git-ignored file. Real mail is not sent to the language model.

Checked by tests: 142 automated backend tests pass, including parallel-ingest, permission and model-loading checks.
